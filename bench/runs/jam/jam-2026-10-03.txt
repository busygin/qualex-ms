3. Findings.
 - Clean optima (rank = span of the solutions: san200_0.7_2, gen200_p0.9_55, 143, 14471, 179*181,
   cubic16): a maximum clique is read off R by linear algebra (decimation in 1-3 steps), projections
   and l4 often work too, and ordering by leverage alone makes the search backtrack-free.
 - Jammed optima (rank far above the span): no direct decoder works.  Along a known solution R_U does
   collapse to its line after 5-9 choices (cubic20, dodecahedron) and 23 on gen200_p0.9_44, but on
   gen200_p0.9_44 wrong paths kept junk directions for 22-28 levels.
 - As pruning inside exact cover search, R cuts the nodes on most SAT01 and QWH instances (241*251:
   23214 -> 309; GP(13,2): 3587 -> 257; saved QWH: 71 of 77 backtrack-free against 58), and on three
   hard QWH instances it found a completion in 532-2250 nodes where plain and leverage-ordered search
   failed within 200000.  But it is erratic in the heavy tail (25,0.40,1: not found in 50000 nodes
   where ordered search needed 8185), each pruned node costs 300-800 plain nodes, and the SDP itself
   takes 20 s to 5.5 min at these sizes.  In wall time it does not beat plain search; verdict
   (Stas): not very impressive.
 - Dense planted clique gen200_p0.9_44 (rank 157, 4 maximum cliques): no gain; only 7 of the 44
   highest-leverage vertices lie in a maximum clique.
 - Numerics: a 1e-5 cut loses the solutions of 241*251 (no spectral gap); restricting the previous
   basis instead of the full one accumulates error and pruned true solutions; the system OpenBLAS
   LAPACK failed to converge once in dgesdd and once in dsyevd on restricted Gram matrices (the second
   ended the 50000-node rerun of 30,0.40,1, which was not repeated).
 - Side finding for the report: the optimum of 179*181 has rank 2, its two solutions; the eigenvalues
   beyond the second (4.9e-5 of the largest and below, decaying smoothly) are the sqrt(mu) tail of
   non-strict complementarity, so Table 8's rank 5 (and wrapper multiplicity 18) are artifacts of the
   1e-5 count.


4. Nonnegative points of the jam (project, added after the above on Stas's proposal).  The global
   minimizers inside R form the sphere S = {x in R: z^T x = 1, |x|^2 = 1/theta}, centre c (the
   least-norm point of R with z^T x = 1), radius rho; every maximum clique indicator lies on it
   (checked to 4e-6), and for a proper Lovasz-optimal wrapper its nonnegative points are exactly the
   maximum cliques.  Starts: the jam vectors c +- rho d_j, d_j the 20 leading eigenvectors of the
   optimum projected into the sphere's directions (as QUALEX-MS takes k vectors from a cluster).
   jam = greedy (by x_i/z_i) on the start itself, which is what QUALEX-MS does now; ap = alternating
   projections (clip the negatives, project back onto S); dr = Douglas-Rachford
   (x += P+(2 P_S x - x) - P_S x, read at P_S x); at most 1000 iterations, stopped at negativity
   below 1e-4 of the norm.  Starts giving a clique of weight theta (and, for ap and dr, starts that

 - Clean optima: ap and dr succeed from every start, and so does greedy on the jam vectors but for 3
   of 24 starts on cubic16 (with rank 1 or 2 the jam vectors are the solutions themselves).
 - Jammed optima: greedy on the jam vectors finds a solution on none of them but gen200_p0.9_44 (1 of
   40).  Douglas-Rachford finds completions on 6 of the 7 jammed QWH instances, from 40 of 40 starts
   on n15_p45_0, n15_p45_1 and the hard 25,0.40,2 (where plain and leverage-ordered Algorithm X failed
   within 200000 nodes), 26 of 40 on n20_p45_2, 6 of 40 on the hard 30,0.40,0 (same), not on
   30,0.42,1 (rank 1247).  Alternating projections are weaker (0-18 of 40) but find completions on all
   QWH instances but 30,0.40,0, among them 30,0.42,1, so the two together solve all seven.  A start
   costs at most about 1 s at order 30.
 - SAT01 instances with contradictions outside the equations (Hamilton cycle, factoring) and the
   dense planted clique: the iterations often reach nonnegative points of S that are not solutions
   (ap 18 of 40 on cubic20 with 2 solutions; dr 21 of 40 on gen200_p0.9_44 with 0), and solutions
   are found only on cubic20 (2-3 of 40).  As predicted, the nonnegative points of the jam are the
   maximum cliques only for a proper wrapper; the Lovasz-optimal jam of these instances is not that
   of a proper wrapper (blind or negative defects; on SAT01, H_A is blind to the 2-clause
   contradictions), while on QCP every contradiction lies in an equation.

   jam vectors give none: 148 of 148, 208 of 208, 139 of 716, 51 of 1144, 772 of 972 on the hard

   gen200_p0.9_44 106 starts end at nonnegative points that are not cliques, the wrapper not being

6. The equation wrapper's jam on the Hamilton cycle instances, with the greedy 2-clause projection
   (project --jam equation --proj orthant|clause; project-clause.log).  The sphere is
   {x in L_A: z^T x = 1, |x|^2 = 1/m}, L_A = D^1/2 {u: Au in R1} the top eigenspace of H_A, computed
   from A alone (no SDP); every exact cover lies on it (checked to 2e-16 on cubic20 and the
   dodecahedron), but H_A is blind to the 2-clause contradictions, so its nonnegative points include
   exact covers that violate them.  The clause projection clips the negatives and keeps the entries in
   decreasing order unless they 2-clash with a kept one; it leaves every solution unchanged, and a
   point of the sphere that it leaves unchanged is a solution.  Starts: all 2(r - 1) corners; at most
   300 iterations; MIN extracts the clique.  Starts that gave a solution (reached: the iterate lies on
   both sets; exact: the projected point is itself a solution, before MIN):

   source                 n   dim starts | jam    DR orthant | AP clause DR clause (reached, exact)
   sat01:cubic16_1       57    29     56 | 54     56         | 56        56 (56, 48)
   sat01:cubic20_1      195   160    318 | 23     11         | 26        190 (175, 187)
   sat01:cubic24_1      403   359    716 | 11     0          | 0         28 (0, 0)
   sat01:dodecahedron   263   227    452 | 11     0          | 7         94 (80, 87)
   sat01:gp13_2         481   433    864 | 0      0          | 0         1 (1, 1)

 - Clipping alone fails on the equation jam: DR reaches no nonnegative point on the three larger
   instances and finds fewer solutions than MIN on the corners (11 against 23 on cubic20, 0 against 11
   on cubic24 and the dodecahedron), as H_A's blindness to the 2-clauses predicts.
 - With the 2-clause projection DR beats the corners on every instance: 190 against 23 starts on
   cubic20, 94 against 11 on the dodecahedron, 28 against 11 on cubic24, 1 against 0 on GP(13,2), and
   where it converges the projected point is itself the solution.  AP with the same projection is far
   weaker.
 - Python iterates each start on its own here (62 s on cubic24, 96 s on GP(13,2)); in C with the
   block products of try_dr_points() the projection onto the sphere would be the cheap part.
