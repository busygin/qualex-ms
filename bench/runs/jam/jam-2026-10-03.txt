3. Findings.
 - Clean optima (rank = span of the solutions: san200_0.7_2, gen200_p0.9_55, 143, 14471, 179*181,
   cubic16): a maximum clique is read off R by linear algebra (decimation in 1-3 steps), projections
   and l4 often work too, and ordering by leverage alone makes the search backtrack-free.
 - Jammed optima (rank far above the span): no direct decoder works.  Along a known solution R_U does
   collapse to its line after 5-9 choices (cubic20, dodecahedron) and 23 on gen200_p0.9_44, but on
   gen200_p0.9_44 wrong paths kept junk directions for 22-28 levels.
 - As pruning inside exact cover search, R cuts the nodes on most SAT01 and QWH instances (241*251:
   23214 -> 309; GP(13,2): 3587 -> 257; saved QWH: 71 of 77 backtrack-free against 58), and on three
   hard QWH instances it found a completion in 532-2250 nodes where plain and leverage-ordered search
   failed within 200000.  But it is erratic in the heavy tail (25,0.40,1: not found in 50000 nodes
   where ordered search needed 8185), each pruned node costs 300-800 plain nodes, and the SDP itself
   takes 20 s to 5.5 min at these sizes.  In wall time it does not beat plain search; verdict
   (Stas): not very impressive.
 - Dense planted clique gen200_p0.9_44 (rank 157, 4 maximum cliques): no gain; only 7 of the 44
   highest-leverage vertices lie in a maximum clique.
 - Numerics: a 1e-5 cut loses the solutions of 241*251 (no spectral gap); restricting the previous
   basis instead of the full one accumulates error and pruned true solutions; the system OpenBLAS
   LAPACK failed to converge once in dgesdd and once in dsyevd on restricted Gram matrices (the second
   ended the 50000-node rerun of 30,0.40,1, which was not repeated).
 - Side finding for the report: the optimum of 179*181 has rank 2, its two solutions; the eigenvalues
   beyond the second (4.9e-5 of the largest and below, decaying smoothly) are the sqrt(mu) tail of
   non-strict complementarity, so Table 8's rank 5 (and wrapper multiplicity 18) are artifacts of the
   1e-5 count.
